# Change management plan: Pega 1:1 Operations Manager rollout (next-best-action decisioning across LOBs)

Prepared 2026-10-13 for the program sponsor (senior marketing executive) and the rollout operations lead. Status: draft from description only. Every score below is inferred from the rollout description; nothing has been measured yet. Replace inference with evidence per section 3 before finalizing.

**Where we are.** The description reads as planning stage, at or just after kickoff, with first LOB go-live around month 6. The plan therefore front-loads the diagnostics (sections 2 to 4) and the sponsor-coalition work; the communications calendar in section 5 is keyed to each LOB wave's go-live so it survives date changes. The technology is not the risk here. The risk is that experienced marketers who built and own their own targeting logic are being asked to hand the targeting decision to a model, in several LOBs whose leaders do not report to the sponsor, in an organization whose last several martech rollouts had mixed adoption.

## 1. The change at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Project** | Enterprise rollout of Pega 1:1 Operations Manager (business-user layer on Pega Customer Decision Hub) at a large US financial services company. Replaces manually built, segment-based campaigns with AI-driven next-best-action decisioning. Rolled out LOB by LOB in waves, not as one cutover. Led by an operations lead with a core team of five coordinating across LOBs. |
| **Purpose** | Personalized, real-time decisioning instead of batch targeting; faster speed to market; stronger marketing-driven revenue; one governance and measurement model across LOBs that today run different processes and tools. Cost of not changing (inferred): continued batch targeting, slower campaign cycles, LOB-by-LOB measurement that cannot be compared, and a decisioning platform that is paid for but bypassed. |
| **Particulars** | Campaigns are no longer authored as segments plus offers. Marketers define actions, treatments, eligibility rules, and objectives; the engine's models and arbitration rules choose the next best action per member, in real time, across channels. New build-approve-deploy workflow with new roles in the approval chain. Results are attributed through the decisioning engine rather than to a hand-built segment. The biggest single change is that targeting judgment moves from the marketer to the model. |
| **People** | Five impacted groups, each repeating in every LOB, tens of people per group per LOB: marketing channel managers (own campaigns in a channel, build targeting today); campaign operations (build, QA, deploy); marketing analysts (measure and report to LOB leadership); LOB marketing leads (teams adopt, accountable for LOB results); compliance approvers (review content and targeting before release). Assumed: 4 to 6 LOBs; 10 to 30 people per group per LOB. |
| **Sponsor** | A senior marketing executive is the primary sponsor. LOB marketing leads report into their own LOB structures, not to the sponsor. Compliance approvers sit outside marketing entirely. No coalition of LOB leaders or a compliance leader is named in the description; that gap is the first judgment call (section 7). |
| **Timeline** | Program kickoff (now, assumed). First LOB go-live around month 6. Further LOBs in waves (cadence not given; assumed one LOB every 2 to 3 months). Business outcomes expected around month 12. |
| **What is algorithmic** | Which action a member sees, when, and in which channel. Previously the marketer decided this through segment design; now the engine's propensity models and arbitration rules decide it per member in real time. People still define actions, treatments, eligibility rules, and objectives, and own the strategy, guardrails, and evaluation. |

### Top three resistance risks

1. **Marketing channel managers** — role identity and loss of decision authority: their expertise was the targeting logic, and the model now makes that decision; without an explicit redefinition of the role and a reviewed override process they will rebuild segments as eligibility rules and override by default — first action: sponsor publishes, and each LOB lead repeats, a one-page statement of what the channel manager role becomes (strategy, action and treatment design, eligibility guardrails, evaluation, exceptions) plus the override policy; channel managers in the wave-1 LOB get named design and side-by-side pilot roles, owner: primary sponsor (statement), people managers (conversation), project team (pilot roles).
2. **LOB marketing leads** — passive sponsorship in later waves, rooted in the fact that they report outside the sponsor's line, measure success their own way today, and have their own Desire barrier (shared governance replaces their process; attribution changes under them) — first action: sponsor convenes the LOB marketing leads and the compliance leader as a named coalition before wave-1 build is locked, and gets each one to agree the same definition of success and the adoption targets in section 6, owner: primary sponsor.
3. **Compliance approvers** — review workflow was designed for human-authored segments and offers; the engine chooses the audience at runtime, and accountability for a model decision is undefined; they will reject or slow approvals, which stalls wave 1 and hands channel managers a legitimate reason to revert — first action: co-design the new approval workflow with compliance (what is approved: eligibility rules, treatments, arbitration guardrails; what evidence the engine provides; who is accountable), and publish the accountability model before wave-1 go-live, owner: project team with the compliance leader.

A close fourth: marketing analysts disputing engine attribution with no crosswalk from the old measures, which lets LOB leadership choose the number they prefer and undermines the revenue case at month 12.

## 6. Adoption metrics

### Definition of adopted

- **Marketing channel managers**: Defines and launches at least 90 percent of new marketing actions in the LOB through 1:1 Operations Manager with no parallel segment build in the legacy tools, with an override or manual-constraint rate within the policy band (assumed under 15 percent of actions, each with a recorded reason), for four consecutive weeks.
- **Campaign operations**: Builds, QA's, and deploys at least 95 percent of the LOB's actions and treatments through the new workflow and approval chain within the agreed cycle time, with no legacy deployments, for four consecutive weeks.
- **Marketing analysts**: Reports LOB campaign performance to LOB leadership from engine attribution only, using the agreed crosswalk, with legacy segment-level reports retired.
- **LOB marketing leads**: Reviews the LOB adoption dashboard weekly, has held a corrective conversation where a team reverted, and has their LOB meeting the three group definitions above by G+8w.
- **Compliance approvers**: Completes at least 95 percent of reviews in the new approval workflow within the agreed turnaround, with no email- or meeting-based approvals of actions or treatments outside the platform.

### Judgment calls only an insider can make

1. **Which LOB marketing leads are actually passive, and which LOB should go first.** The plan assumes wave 1 goes to an LOB whose lead is an active Supporter. If the wave order was set by technical convenience or by whichever LOB had budget, the first wave may be the hardest one, and the whole program will read its prospects from it.
2. **Whether use is mandated and the legacy path will really be retired, LOB by LOB.** Everything in Ability and Reinforcement depends on the old segment tools going away on a date. Only the sponsor and the LOB executives know whether that is enforceable.
3. **Whether the sponsor can and will reach into LOBs they do not own.** The LOB marketing leads report elsewhere and compliance sits outside marketing. The sponsor must either build the coalition personally or get the LOB executives and the compliance leader to co-sponsor; the core team of five cannot substitute for this.
4. **What the real history is.** "Mixed adoption" could mean a failed tool that was quietly abandoned or a successful one that took two years. The Desire prior and the trust messaging depend on which.
5. **How much override authority channel managers should keep.** Too little and the role statement is hollow and Desire never clears; too much and the engine never gets the chance to show lift. The policy band is a business decision for the sponsor with data science and compliance, not a change-management decision.
6. **Whether compliance will accept approving rules and guardrails instead of a frozen audience list, and what evidence they need from the engine to do so.** If the answer is no, the approval chain and the real-time promise both need redesign before wave 1.
7. **What the channel manager role is measured on once the engine decides targeting.** If LOB incentives still reward segment-level results, the role statement will be contradicted by the scorecard, and the scorecard will win.
8. **Who owns decisioning governance, override review, and the adoption dashboard after the core team moves to the next wave.** Naming this person is a sponsor decision that the plan cannot make.

1. **Sponsor conversation with a specific ask (this week).** The change lead presents the sponsor with the coalition gap, the three judgment calls above that only the sponsor can settle (wave-1 LOB and its lead, legacy retirement authority, operational owner), and the request for one-on-ones with each LOB marketing lead and the compliance leader plus a standing weekly check-in.
2. **Replace inference with evidence (within three weeks).** Five-question pulse through managers in the wave-1 LOB; manager interviews with channel managers' managers; one conversation each with the analytics leads and the compliance leader; rebuild the coalition table and re-score section 3 from the results.
3. **Lock the three artifacts that clear the Desire barriers before any training is scheduled (by G-10w for wave 1).** The channel manager role statement, the override policy, and the compliance-approved accountability model and approval workflow; alongside them, get the definitions of adopted and the adoption targets in section 6 signed by the coalition and added to the project plan next to the go-live dates.
